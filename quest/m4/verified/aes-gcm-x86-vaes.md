# [XL] AES-GCM on x86_64 with VAES is proven

## Goal

AES-GCM earns the ✓ on x86_64 CPUs with VAES and VPCLMULQDQ (Ice Lake and later, Zen 3 and later), the common tier on today's x86_64 servers and desktops: BoringSSL's VAES kernels, and rind's choice among them, Vale's, and its own, are proven.

## Plan

- Runs (`low/x86_64/aes_gcm_vaes.rs`): BoringSSL's `aes_gcm_{enc,dec}_update_vaes_avx2` from 256 bytes, and `aes_gcm_{enc,dec}_update_vaes_avx512` from 2 KB where the CPU has AVX-512, with their key-power setup `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx2` and `_avx512` in BoringSSL's table layout; below those lengths, rind's by-8 and AVX-512 by-16 intrinsics (proven, given this tier's key powers), and Vale's proven routines for opens under 256 bytes on cores without AVX-512.
- Already proven: Vale's routines, the AEAD API's glue on Vale's tier, and nothing of BoringSSL's. No published proof of a VAES or AVX-512 AES-GCM routine exists, and Vale has none.
- Route: Lean proofs of BoringSSL's four kernels and two key setups on VG's x86_64 model, with their leakage contracts, as [kixel-ai/rind#912](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/912) proved aarch64 SHA-256. VG's model already has EVEX `vaesenc`, `vaesenclast`, and `vpclmulqdq`; model what the kernels use beyond it, such as the VEX.256 forms, in `proofs/verified-garbage/RindIsa/`. List each program in `RindIsa/Proven.lean`, so `admin/prove lean` checks its bytes and `admin/prove vg` cosimulates it, as `bn_mul4x_mont`'s are. `rind_isa` fails on any relocation in an x86_64 function today, and these kernels load their constant tables RIP-relative, so first extend it to check each relocation's target and the table's bytes, as aarch64's check does for `adrp` and `add`.
- The spec: `common/gcm.ml` and `common/fips197.ml` at rind's s2n-bignum pin, `3f01260f`, which has [awslabs/s2n-bignum#490](https://github.com/awslabs/s2n-bignum/pull/490)'s rewrite of both and their known-answer tests (`common/gcm_kats.ml`, `common/fips197_kats.ml`).
- Then Aeneas to Lean for rind's glue around the kernels and the length-based choice, as `Rind/X86_64/AesGcm.lean` does for Vale's tier.
- The two GCM specs (maintainer, 2026-11-09): ✅ prove them equal in Lean (rejected: amend the composition rule to allow a spec-equivalence axiom). Done, with no axiom beyond Lean's own: `Rind/S2nGcm.lean` ([kixel-ai/rind#1045](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/1045)) proves `aes128_cipher` and `aes256_cipher` Vale's `eval_cipher`, the kernels' counter blocks and keystream Vale's, and `nist_ghash` Vale's GHASH, and `Rind/S2nGcmAe.lean` proves `common/gcm.ml`'s `gctr` Vale's from any counter block (`gctr_eq`), `gcm_ae` and `gcm_ad` with a 96-bit IV and whole blocks `gcm_encrypt_LE` and `gcm_decrypt_LE` for any cipher that is Vale's AES under the key (`gcmAe_eq`, `gcmAd_eq`), and `common/fips197.ml`'s AES-128 and AES-256 key schedules Vale's (`aes128KeyExpansion_eq`, `aes256KeyExpansion_eq`, and `gcmAe_aes128` to `gcmAd_aes256` end to end).
  - s2n-bignum's spec has no AES-192 (`common/fips197.ml` defines `aes128_cipher` and `aes256_cipher` only), but BoringSSL's VAES kernels take AES-192 keys. `gcmAe_eq` and `gcmAd_eq` take any cipher that is Vale's AES, so an AES-192 kernel theorem over `gcm_ae` needs only an `aes192_cipher` and its key schedule beside them; stating that kernel's theorem over Vale's `gctr_encrypt_LE` and a GHASH from the supplied counter block and accumulator (`ghashPoly` seeded with the incoming accumulator: both dispatch arms hash the AAD before the kernel, so it can be nonzero, which `ghash_LE` from zero cannot describe), then composing those into `gcm_encrypt_LE`, needs neither. A kernel call is an update step from a state, not a whole message from the IV, so `gcm_encrypt_LE` alone does not express it.
  - The kernels are update steps, GCTR from a counter block and GHASH from an accumulator, which `gctr_eq` and `nistGhash_eq` state from any. `gctr` takes whole blocks: so does the AVX2 kernel, but the AVX-512 one masks a partial last block (`low/x86_64/aes_gcm_vaes.rs:90`), so its theorem needs a spec past `gctr`'s, such as Vale's `gctr_encrypt_LE` directly.
- The AVX-512 tier below 2 KB is proven over two predicates this quest discharges for `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx512`: `Avx512Powers H t`, that `VaesKernel::powers` gives the hash key's 16 descending powers, and `TableFor H t.clmul`, that the PCLMULQDQ table `from_powers` builds holds the first eight. Given them, `tagged_from_avx512_spec` (`proofs/Rind/X86_64/AesGcmAvx512.lean`) is `tagged_from_spec`'s ciphertext and tag under 2 KB, by-16 from 256 bytes and by-8 below, so the tier's seals and opens compose it with the kernel's proof from 2 KB, as `Rind/X86_64/AesGcmClmul.lean` composes `tagged_from_spec`.
- [kixel-ai/rind#955](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/955) measured pyca/verified-garbage's AVX-512 kernels under rind's glue and kept BoringSSL's, so AVX-512 CPUs' long messages stay this quest's to prove.
