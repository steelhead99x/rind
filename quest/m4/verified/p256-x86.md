# [L] P-256 ECDH and ECDSA are proven on x86_64

## Goal

P-256 key agreement, ECDSA signing, and ECDSA verification, which every TLS certificate chain with an ECDSA key exercises, earn the ✓ on x86_64, as they do on aarch64.

## Plan

- Done in [kixel-ai/rind#648](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/648): P-256 agreement, `p256_agree` in `Rind/X86_64/P256Agree.lean`, which `admin/prove` also writes for aarch64, down to s2n-bignum's `p256_scalarmul` and its `_alt` form, which d·Q runs on both targets. On aarch64, all the shared Rust: key generation in `Rind/Aarch64/P256.lean` and ECDSA in `Rind/Aarch64/P256Ecdsa.lean` and `P256Signature.lean`, whose lemmas already take x86_64's `Runs`.
- Remaining runs, all for k·G (`base_multiply`, opaque in x86_64's translation): the select of a row's affine point, and aws-lc's `ecp_nistz256_point_add_affine` or its BMI2 and ADX form `_affinex` with their field helpers, under rind's Booth-7 driver over a 152 KB table of 37 rows of 64 affine points (`mid/p256.rs`, `low/x86_64/ecp_nistz256.rs`). Verification's u1·G reads the same table.
- Decided 2026-11-12 (maintainer, /quest-unblock): ✅ k·G runs rind's Rust select, `bignum_point_select`'s `select_avx2::<8, 2>` and `select_sse2::<8, 4>`, which P-384 and P-521 share and Lean proves through intrinsic models, accepting about 2.4% on key generation and nothing on signing (rejected: keep aws-lc's `select_w7` and `avx2_select_w7` and prove them in HOL Light after extending s2n-bignum's model with PCMPEQD, VPCMPEQD, VPERMD, VMOVD, and PREFETCHT0). One select proof serves all three curves, and fewer model extensions enter the trust base.
- s2n-bignum's routines are no faster route for the rest of k·G: its proven x86_64 `p256_scalarmulbase` cost 12-17% ([kixel-ai/rind#234](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/234)), and its `p256_montjmixadd` under rind's driver cost key generation 13% (a 6-bit table, signing 8%, or a 7-bit one).
- Route:
  1. Replace `p256_select_w7`'s aws-lc routines with a `bignum_aff_point_select_p256` beside P-384's and P-521's in `low/x86_64/bignum_point_select.rs`, delete `ecp_nistz256_select_w7` and `avx2_select_w7`, and benchmark key generation and signing against aws-lc-rs to confirm the measured cost.
  2. Prove the select through the intrinsic models of `select_avx2` and `select_sse2` that [P-384's quest](/quest/m4/verified/p384.md) writes to discharge its `AffSelect`, tested by `admin/simd-vectors.rs`, instantiated at P-256's `<8, 2>` and `<8, 4>`. Whichever quest gets there first writes the generic models.
  3. HOL Light proofs of aws-lc's mixed addition in s2n-bignum's x86_64 model against its P-256 specs, extending rind's additions to the x86 model in its fork, `thirdparty/s2n-bignum`, which [kixel-ai/rind#627](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/627) started with POR, with any instructions it uses that the model lacks.
  4. Aeneas for the Booth driver, and a proof that the x86_64 table holds the multiples of G those proofs assume, as `Rind/Aarch64/P256Table.lean` does for aarch64's, reusing the pattern of P-384's table proof (`Rind/X86_64/P384Table.lean`, [kixel-ai/rind#654](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/654)) if it has landed.
  5. Move key generation and ECDSA's proofs from `Rind/Aarch64/` into `Rind/X86_64/`, for `admin/prove` to copy back, as agreement's are, with the x86_64 axioms ECDSA adds to `Rind/X86_64/S2nBignum.lean` (`p256_montjadd`, `p256_montjdouble`, `p256_montjmixadd`, `bignum_inv_p256`, `bignum_neg_p256`, `bignum_mod_n256`, and the ADX routines' `_alt` forms), and add the x86_64 rows to `benchmarks/verified.toml`.
