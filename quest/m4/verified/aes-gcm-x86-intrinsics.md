# [L] x86_64's AVX-512 by-16 AES-GCM is proven

## Goal

rind's last x86_64 AES-GCM intrinsics path is proven: the AVX-512 by-16 AES-CTR and GHASH, which run messages from 256 bytes to 2 KB on CPUs with AVX-512, VAES, and VPCLMULQDQ, so every input rind takes on CPUs with AES-NI, PCLMULQDQ, SSSE3, and SSE4.1 (an AES-NI CPU without SSSE3 runs the unverified `ghash_nohw`) reaches a proof once [the VAES quest](/quest/m4/verified/aes-gcm-x86-vaes.md) proves BoringSSL's kernels and key setup.

## Plan

- Done ([kixel-ai/rind#633](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/633)): the by-8 AES-CTR and GHASH, the PCLMULQDQ table, the counter, J0 for 96-bit IVs, the length block, the tag, and the AEAD API's `seal` and `open` on the SSE tier and Vale's, against `Rind/Vale.lean`'s `gcm_encrypt_LE` and `gcm_decrypt_LE` without their 2^32 bound.
- Done ([kixel-ai/rind#703](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/703)): `aead::gcm::Key`'s IVs of any length and truncated tags, through `encrypt_any`, `decrypt_any`, and `initial_counter` (`Rind/X86_64/AesGcmAny.lean`), with their refusals of empty or 2^61-byte IVs and of AAD and messages past GCM's bounds; and AES-192 keys, by-8 on both tiers, in the `AesGcm`, AEAD, HKDF, and `gcm::Key` theorems. Vale's routines never run AES-192 (`vale_192_none`), so the Vale-tier lemmas, such as `aes_gcm_encrypt_vale`, still take AES-128 and AES-256 alone.
- AVX-512 by-16: model `_cipher_avx512`, `Counter512`, `GhashTableAvx512::add`, `_mul16`, and `_mul_tail` with the 512-bit intrinsics, extending `Rind/X86_64/Simd.lean`, with `admin/simd-vectors.rs` checking each new intrinsic's model on an AVX-512 CPU. Prove them against the same specs as by-8, `gctrEncrypt` and `gcmHash`, given a GHASH table whose 16 descending powers are the hash key's, a predicate the theorem takes.
- The key powers: `GhashTableAvx512`'s powers, and the `ClmulTable` `from_powers` builds from them for the tier's by-8, come from `VaesKernel::avx512`, BoringSSL's `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx512`, which the VAES quest proves. So this quest states by-16 and the tier's by-8 over that predicate, and the VAES quest discharges it and composes the tier's dispatch, which also runs its kernels from 2 KB. That breaks the cycle of each quest needing the other.
- Refusals: `AesGcm::encrypt_any` and `decrypt_any` already refuse AAD of 2^61 bytes or more and messages past 2^36 - 32 bytes with `WrongLength`, leaving the buffer as it was. The 96-bit `AesGcm::encrypt` and `decrypt`, and the AEAD and `gcm::Key` theorems above them, still take those bounds as hypotheses; stating their refusals (`check_gcm_lengths_eq` gives them) would make them total.
